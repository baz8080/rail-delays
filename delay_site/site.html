Newest first, by when the notice was first listed.

A notice going up is the only signal there is. Irish Rail publishes no history and no "resolved" field, so a disruption is here from the poll its notice first appeared on, and nothing here says when the trains were running normally again.

What this measures

The same feed of service messages the sibling lift site reads, polled every 30 minutes, minus the lift and escalator outages, which are published there. Nothing before exists here, and days before it are shown as "no data".

Trains listed as having reduced capacity are left out. "Customer Notice: This train has reduced capacity" is a template about the seats rather than about a train running late, and on the corpus to September 2026 it was a third of the feed from two distinct headlines. The count is printed above each month's list so it is not quietly missing.

One disruption is one event, not one notice. Irish Rail re-words a live notice as things develop - nine wordings over eight hours on 17 August were one closure between Thurles and Limerick Junction - and those are folded into one entry that says how many times it was re-worded.

Why there are no totals in minutes

Some notices carry a figure and most do not, and the ones that do are not the same kind of number: "this service departed +25 minutes behind schedule" is one train observed, while "+90 minute delays can be expected" is a forecast over many. Adding them up would also count one event several times, because the figure is re-stated every time the notice is re-worded.

So each disruption shows the worst figure its notice ever claimed and nothing is summed. Where the feed writes a range, the lower end is shown, which is why it says "at least".

Why there is no percentage

Irish Rail names a service only when something is wrong with it. There is no published roll of the services that ran, so there is nothing to divide by: this site can say how many notices were listed and never what share of trains were late. Any such figure would be invented.

How a cause is read

Each notice usually names a cause in its own words - "due to a signalling issue", "following an incident on the line". Those words are read into a set of categories drawn from what the feed actually writes, and a notice can name more than one.

Nothing is decoded. "An incident on the line" is published as that and no more, because the notice says no more. "An operational issue" is filed as no cause given, because that is what it is. And a notice blaming another delay is marked as doing so rather than counted beside a fault.
